# nnormalize

A browser tool that reads any photo, finds what is wrong with it — flat contrast, the glow and outlines that make a subject look pasted on, smeared texture, blur, repeating patterns, block edges, uneven grain, colour blotches, dead pixels — fixes only those things, lets you dial every fix down globally or by area, and writes a JPEG that holds up after the place you post it re-saves it.

Everything runs locally in the browser. Nothing is uploaded.

The hosted copy is at the repository's GitHub Pages address; `nnormalize-single.html` is the same tool in one file that opens from a folder.

## What it does

1. **Reads the photo at full precision.** JPEG through mozjpeg (the same decoder it uses to check its own output), PNG including 16-bit through its own decoder, HEIC through libheif where the browser can't, everything else through the browser. EXIF orientation is applied to the pixels; the camera metadata and colour profile are kept for the output.
2. **Looks for problems, each measured in plain numbers.** "Levels" throughout are brightness steps out of 255: 10 is obvious, 1 is faint, 0.3 is invisible.
   - **Repeating patterns** (moiré, scan lines, screens, banding): spikes in the averaged spectrum of overlapping tiles, measured to a fraction of a bin, then their strength and phase measured on every tile, so the tool knows where in the frame each pattern lives and whether it is the same everywhere. Harmonics are grouped under their fundamental. Line patterns on the spectrum axes are judged against the ring's own spread once they are 12 dB clear, so banding next to straight scene edges is still found.
   - **Patterns that drift across the frame** (a screen photographed at an angle, a scanned print, a fabric beating against the sensor). In any small patch these are clean stripes, but their spacing and direction change smoothly from one part of the frame to another, so the averaged spectrum above smears them into nothing. They are found window by window instead, the way fringe patterns with a varying frequency are handled in optical metrology (windowed Fourier filtering; Kemao 2004, 2007): the photo is read in 256-px windows overlapping by half; in each, a spike that stands well above the window's own radial background and is isolated (a straight edge makes a ridge, not a spike: the spike must clear all but the top four of the 120 bins 4–6 bins away) is a candidate, located to a fraction of a bin; candidates in neighbouring windows at nearly the same frequency are linked, and a chain that spans enough windows is a pattern, while a lone spike is texture or chance. The chain's smoothly drifting frequency field is what tells interference from scene texture: it runs on across skin, cloth and wall alike. Fixed patterns too weak or too local for the whole-photo spectrum are caught the same way, down to a 2-px period. Reported in plain numbers: spacing range, direction and how far it turns, typical and strongest amplitude, share of the photo.
   - **Block edges** from an earlier JPEG save: the grid phase, the step in levels, and an estimate of the quality it was saved at.
   - **Grain and uneven grain.** Grain is read as the fine residual each 32-px block carries, per brightness. The photo's base grain is the lowest substantial cluster of plain blocks (so a photo that is mostly fine texture still measures its grain from the plain parts), unless a clearly larger cluster lies nearby. Texture is told from grain three ways: aligned gradients (edges, weave), strength far above the grain, and spatial correlation redder than the grain's — stronger noise comes through compression whiter, never redder, so a grainier patch is still grain and sand or fabric is not. Nothing has to be masked: flat areas and grainier patches are found on their own.
   - **Colour blotches:** colour that brightness does not explain (the residual after luma-guided smoothing), measured at two scales so soft blobs count.
   - **Halos next to edges:** the fine residual in the band beside strong edges, clear of the edge's own transition, against the grain in plain blocks.
   - **Dead and stuck pixels.**
   - **Flat, low contrast.** Phones compress a scene's range so hard that shadows sit well above black and neighbouring areas barely differ. Measured as how far the darkest shadows sit above black and how much neighbouring areas differ, against what the same scene shows when its range is not squeezed. Fixed with a local Laplacian filter (Paris, Hasinoff & Kautz 2011; the fast version of Aubry et al. 2014) run at reduced size and brought back with guided upsampling: it raises local contrast between areas without touching fine grain and without halos at edges, sets the black point with a soft toe, and rolls off near white. Brightness only; colours are kept.
   - **Glow around subjects:** the wide bright/dark band, several to tens of pixels wide, that exposure fusion leaves along strong edges (the low-frequency halo; Hessel 2019). Read across every strong straight edge out to 48 px; where a side is smooth, its real level and slope are fitted from 30–48 px out, and a band that exaggerates the edge and fades with distance is flattened back onto that fit. Sides with texture or other edges are left alone, and the fix only acts when the glow shows on a good share of the photo's smooth edge sides.
   - **Smeared fine texture.** Natural scenes carry predictable detail at each scale (power falling as 1/f²; Torralba & Oliva 2003), so wherever a patch has texture at one scale, the next finer scale should carry a predictable share of it. Where it carries less, noise reduction flattened it, and the photo's own remaining fine detail is strengthened toward that share (at most ×2.5). Nothing is invented; flat areas and strong edges are left alone.
   - **Camera blur and sharpening outlines**, measured from the photo's straight edges. Real edges are sharp steps, so the width and the bright or dark outline a photo shows at its sharpest edges were added by the camera. Hundreds of straight, isolated edges are sampled across at quarter-pixel steps and aligned, and the sharpest fifth give the camera's edge profile (the slanted-edge idea; Joshi, Szeliski & Kriegman 2008). That profile is fitted with a blurred step plus sharpening at three widths (phones sharpen as a sum of Gaussians), which is undone exactly; the blur is then reduced (regularised toward leaving a frequency as it is, ×6 at most) only as far as it can go without creating new outlines. At strong edges 45 % of the camera's crispness is kept by default (adjustable); in texture all of it is kept and only the blur is reduced. Fine frequencies are raised only where there is real detail: real detail carries at least as much energy one scale up as at the finest scale, noise far less, so skies and walls are not roughened. A sharpening threshold then fades what the boost adds wherever it is the size of noise. Lopsided outlines beside flat areas (sky, wall, skin), which a linear filter cannot reach, are pulled back to that area's own level; edges with texture on both sides keep their texture. This replaces an earlier check that judged fine detail against the photo's own coarser detail, which a camera that softens everything can fool.
   - **False colour on fine detail:** colour speckles, zippers and fringes left where the camera guessed the missing colours wrong. Measured as colour that does not follow brightness within 5×5 pixels on fine detail, against the same in smooth areas; fixed by pulling colour on fine detail onto what its brightness predicts (a red letter on white keeps its colour).
   - **Banding in smooth areas:** steps in skies, walls and shadows, found on the stored 8-bit values (smooth blocks, almost no grain, flat plateaus between regular small steps). The fix smooths only those areas back into a ramp with an edge-keeping filter wider than the bands, and adds a fine dither. How visible banding is gets measured along the bands, with grain cancelled out, so added grain neither hides nor fakes it.
3. **Fixes each one as a separate layer, in order.** A stationary pattern is subtracted exactly from its fitted parameters; one that varies is subtracted tile by tile; spectral flatten and notch are there when you want them. A drifting pattern is removed window by window: in each 256-px window only the narrow band of frequencies around the tracked spike (±3 bins) is taken out, each bin scaled by how far it stands above that window's own background (the optimum-notch idea; Gonzalez & Woods), and the windows are blended back with their Hann weights. Everything outside that sliver of the spectrum is left exactly as it was — no blur, no smoothing — and the test suite holds it: on planted stripes the finest detail of the scene is unchanged to 1 %. All three colour channels are treated, so coloured moiré goes with the brightness stripes; the fix reaches two windows past the last place the pattern was tracked, which is safe because the notch only removes what stands above the background. Block edges get a grid-aligned deblocker (over four pixels, or the whole block at the stronger levels). Grain is levelled down toward the base grain with an edge-preserving smoother whose strength is measured per block, not assumed (cleaner patches are left clean; no synthetic grain is ever added); textured blocks only ever get the weakest filters, and less of them. Colour blotches are smoothed toward the luma-guided colour. Halos get an edge-preserving smoother capped so strong edges stay sharp, run after the edge fix so it also catches anything sharpening raised beside edges. Contrast, glow, texture and false colour are changed in brightness or colour only, never both. Outliers are replaced by their neighbours. Each later stage is computed on the image with the earlier stages applied, so a moiré is never mistaken for block steps or grain.
4. **Lets you tone it down.** A master strength, a strength per fix, and a brush per fix painted directly on the photo (amber shows where a fix is held back), seeded from where the analysis found the pattern or from a brightness range. Masks are always feathered.
5. **Writes the file for where it is going.** *Reddit and sharing apps*: quality 75, 4:2:0, the standard tables, and the file is encoded repeatedly until a re-save reproduces it byte for byte — so Reddit's own re-encode changes nothing. Block edges are also written on that quality's coefficient grid: each block's coefficients are nudged by at most one step where that makes it meet its neighbours smoothly, so the re-save cannot bring the steps back. *Master copy*: quality 95, full colour, mozjpeg's tuned tables. *Custom*: your own settings.
6. **Bakes the result through a camera sensor (on by default).** At the very end the finished picture is re-sampled to one colour per pixel on the photo's own sensor grid (the green lattice is read from the photo; red/blue placement is assumed), in linear light, and the missing colours are filled in again with Malvar–He–Cutler, so every edit carries one consistent sensor signature. *Clean* (the default) adds no noise at all. *Sharp* adds subtle photon noise at each photosite (variance proportional to the light, plus a little read noise); *Match the original's grid* uses bilinear filling-in when needed and enough noise that the grid shows, once the file is written at the destination's quality, about as clearly as the original's would. A quality-75 re-save (Reddit) erases any camera's grid, the original's too; quality 85 and up keep it, and the Master copy now uses standard tables without trellis so it does.
7. **Learns the sensor's own signature and can put it back (PRNU).** Every photosite has its own gain, a fraction of a percent off its neighbours' and fixed for life; that pattern rides on every photo the camera takes and is what forensic tools use to tell cameras apart. Clean-up wears it down a little and a low-quality re-save wears it away. The tool learns it from the photos that pass through, following the forensics literature (Lukáš, Fridrich & Goljan 2006; the maximum-likelihood estimator of Chen et al. 2008; the Binghamton reference implementation): each photo's noise residual, weighted by brightness and flatness so smooth well-lit areas count most and clipped or busy ones not at all, is folded into a running estimate per camera and frame size (from the EXIF make, model and dimensions); rows and columns are zero-meaned and a Wiener filter in the frequency domain strips the spikes every camera of the model shares (the JPEG grid, the colour-filter lattice, readout stripes). Reliability is measured, not assumed: a split-half pair on a centre window gives the correlation between two independent halves of the batch, and Spearman–Brown turns that into the reliability of the whole; the test suite plants a 0.5 % pattern and recovers it at 0.89 correlation from 24 flat frames with a self-measured reliability of 0.83, while a photo from another camera reads z ≈ 0. Any photo you load teaches it; a batch of 30–50 defocused shots of plain walls teaches it in minutes ("Learn from photos…"). The map lives in the browser's own storage, per camera, and can be exported and imported as a file. When ticked, the finished picture is multiplied by the pattern, scaled by its reliability (the least-error weighting of a noisy estimate) and calibrated on a centre window so the photo carries as much of the signature as it did untouched; the live check reports the correlation with the map in the untouched photo, the result, the file and the posted copy. Photos must be straight from the camera at full size: anything re-saved by a chat app or social site has had the signature stripped (a quality-75 re-save removes it almost entirely), so those neither teach it nor carry it.
8. **Checks what a downloader gets, live.** A strip under the photo re-runs the check a moment after every change (strength, ticks, brush, level, destination): the centre 1536 px of the photo (or all of it, when smaller) is written at the destination's settings, put through its re-save, and every ticked fix measured against the untouched photo given the same re-save. Saving runs the same check on the whole photo.

   The full check: The file it wrote is put through the destination's re-save, and so is the untouched photo; every fix is then measured on both copies and reported as kept / partly / lost, in levels. The *As posted* view shows the downloader's copy, and Split puts the untouched photo through the same re-save beside it.

## Running it

```
npm install
npm run dev          # local development server (dev.html)
npm test             # unit tests: FFT, sub-bin refinement, corrections, noise and texture, block grid and block settling, hot pixels, colour, halos, engine end-to-end
npm run build:pages  # dist/ plus the copy served from the repository root, and the single-file build
```

The site is served from the repository root on the main branch, so `npm run build:pages` must be run before committing a change to the tool; the workflow in `.github/workflows/pages.yml` rebuilds it on every push as well.

## Using it

Drop a photo, or load the test image, which plants three known patterns, an 8×8 grid and a dozen hot pixels so you can see what the numbers look like when the answer is known.

The side panel is the workflow, in four numbered steps: **1 Photo** (open or drop one), **2 How far, and where it is going**, **3 Problems found** (tick, untick, click to adjust), **4 Save and check**. The panel can be dragged wider. The spectrum, analysis thresholds, recipes and batch sit under *More* at the bottom.

- **Clean-up** (step 2): *Gentle* and *Standard* fix what is clearly wrong (contrast, glow, outlines, texture at moderate strength) and leave the grain alone apart from bringing heavier patches down. *Strong* and *Maximum* push contrast and texture further, also remove grain everywhere (50 % / 80 %), blend block edges over the whole block, and smooth colour blotches and halos harder.
- **Going to** (step 2): Reddit and sharing apps, master copy, or custom. In step 4, "Save JPEG and check it" writes the file and runs the check; "Check only" runs the check without downloading.
- **Views:** before, after, what changed (amplified by the slider), as posted. Split compares before and after, or the downloader's copy against the untouched photo's. Fit, 100 % and 200 % zoom; double-click toggles fit and 100 %.
- **Brush:** with a problem selected, paint on the picture where its fix should be held back. "Only where it was found" and the brightness range seed the mask automatically.
- **Spectrum:** hover for exact frequency, period, angle, lines per inch when the DPI is known, and dB over background. Click a ring to select that finding; "Add a peak by clicking" turns a click into a new finding measured like the rest.
- **A selected problem** opens its controls right under it: strength, method, and under "Hold it back in places" the brush, "Where it was found" and a brightness range. Technical numbers sit under *Measurements*.
- **Recipes:** save the findings, strengths, methods, masks, level and destination as JSON; load one to apply the same choices to another photo (patterns are matched by frequency). Batch runs a set of photos through analysis, recipe, export and check, downloading each result and a summary report.

## Precision

The claims the tool makes are measured, not assumed, and the test suite holds them: frequency to better than 0.001 cycles per pixel, amplitude to within 1 % from the whole image, phase to within 0.05 rad; a planted stationary pattern is removed to within 0.3 dB of the noise floor; a planted non-stationary one is flagged as such and the tiled subtraction beats the global one; planted stripes whose spacing drifts from 10 to 7 px and whose direction turns by 15° are tracked across the frame and removed to under 15 % of their strength with the scene's finest detail unchanged to 1 %, a 2.5-px grid is removed to under 0.2 levels, and a scene with hard edges and a small striped patch reports no pattern; planted grain is recovered within 5 %, texture is scored high and grain low, a grainier patch is still grain, colour blotches are halved, halos are smoothed with nothing changed away from edges, and settled block boundaries are a fixed point of the destination's quantiser. The check then repeats the measurements on the downloader's copy.

## Not checked

Dust, hairs and scratches larger than a pixel; lens flare and chromatic aberration; focus and motion blur (the camera's own softness is handled; a missed focus is not); blown highlights; colour casts.

## Layout

```
src/dsp       fft, windows, tiles (Welch), background model, peaks, refine (sub-bin DTFT), pattern (per-tile measurement),
              drift (patterns whose frequency drifts: windowed tracking and notch),
              filter (parametric and spectral corrections), noise (grain, texture, levelling), chroma (colour blotches, halos),
              tone (local Laplacian contrast, black point), glow (exposure-fusion halo), texture (fine-detail recovery),
              edges (camera edge model, outline clamp, detail mask), cfa (sensor grid, demosaic, re-bake, false colour),
              prnu (sensor signature: residual, maximum-likelihood estimate, clean-up, reliability, calibration),
              blocks (grid forensics, deblock), jpegdomain (block settling on the destination's coefficient grid), hotpixels
src/io        decode (mozjpeg, PNG16, HEIC, native), jpegParse (segments, quant tables, metadata splice), exif, png, encode (mozjpeg, fixed point)
src/analysis  types, analyze (findings), apply (delta layers), survival (what a downloader gets)
src/engine    engine (state, layers, compositing, export, check), profiles (destinations), worker protocol, client,
              signature (the PRNU bank: learning, storage, export/import)
src/ui        app, viewer, spectrum panel, presets, words (plain language)
src/synth     synthetic images with known ground truth
tests         vitest
```
